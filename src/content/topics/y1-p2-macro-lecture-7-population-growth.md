![Lecture slide: in the steady state growth stops and output, capital, output per person and consumption per person are all constant.](../../assets/notes/year1/1cb349d811457d958d0f.png)

If we are assuming the labour supply is fixed that is.

When solving such questions always begin with the **fundamental equation**:

![Lecture slide: with population and the labour force growing at a constant exogenous rate n, the fundamental equation in aggregate levels is ΔK = sF(K,L) − δK.](../../assets/notes/year1/c9759055e76b33ab7d41.png)

So that (by making use of the constant returns of C-D production):

![Lecture derivation: dividing the aggregate equation by L gives ΔK/L = sf(k) − δk in per capita terms.](../../assets/notes/year1/8c04ab04340356c9d270.png)

![Lecture derivation: treating Δ as an approximation to a derivative, Δk = ΔK/L − k(ΔL/L), where ΔL/L is the population growth rate n.](../../assets/notes/year1/d3f7d3843cb101b285ab.png)

![Lecture slide: equation of motion with population growth, Δk = sf(k) − (δ + n)k, separating actual investment from break-even investment.](../../assets/notes/year1/c17aa9e348b9b734b30d.png)

## Intuition

![Lecture slide: in the steady state break-even investment (δ + n)k keeps k constant — δk replaces worn-out capital and nk equips new workers with capital.](../../assets/notes/year1/25a898267d3ee536ad37.png)
